Overview

Research question, hypothesis, and project status at a glance.

Research question

Does the quality (completeness, timeliness, diagnostic accuracy) of South Africa’s civil registration and vital statistics (CRVS) data distort how health resource allocation is calibrated across programmes, particularly for causes outside HIV/TB (which are modelled separately via the Thembisa epidemiological model and are out of scope here)? This question has not been answered by this project yet.

Working hypothesis

CRVS data-quality issues — incomplete registration, delayed reporting, diagnostic misclassification of underlying cause of death — may distort how provincial and national health budgets are allocated across programmes. This is the question this pipeline is built to investigate, not a conclusion already reached. It is distinct from existing South African literature that asks whether health spending affects outcomes directly: this project sits upstream of that question, asking whether the spending itself is calibrated against burden estimates already known to be wrong.

Two mechanisms documented in the general CRVS/ICD-coding literature plausibly explain part of this data-quality gap (not yet tested against this project’s own data): an unresolved tension between patient confidentiality and public health surveillance use of HIV-related death data, and non-specific (“ill-defined”) diagnoses written by certifying clinicians that central coders cannot reliably translate into ICD codes regardless of coder skill — see the Plan & Open Questions chapter for the two preliminary, non-committal recommendation directions these mechanisms suggest.

Status at a glance

  • Budget line items pulled: 42,578, covering 9 provinces and financial years 2020–2026 (National Treasury EPRE, 2024-25 release).
  • Citation matches found: 320, across 9 real National DoH strategic documents.
  • Programme-name variants flagged and corrected: 4 of 72 province × programme combinations audited.
  • Not yet built: mortality-burden panel, funding-gap computation (see the Plan & Open Questions chapter).

These numbers come directly from data/processed/ and data/crosswalks/, produced by scripts/run_first_implementer.R — nothing on this page is a research finding.

How this project is organised

  • Budget Panel — explore the real provincial Health-department budget.
  • Programme Crosswalk & Data Quality — how raw programme-name spelling variants were audited and corrected.
  • Citation Trail — which burden-of-disease data sources are actually cited in national strategic planning documents.
  • Plan & Open Questions — the working hypothesis, what’s built vs. not, and open design questions.
  • CPHIA Conference Abstract (Draft) — a conceptual, introductory abstract framing this work for the academic community, not a results abstract.

Full detail lives in the project’s living docs, not duplicated here: docs/KNOWLEDGE.md (confirmed facts only) and docs/PLAN.md (hypothesis, plan, open questions).
