Cross-Country Landscape

What evidence informs national health budgets and strategic plans, compared across D4H countries.
Note

Status: all planned countries populated, now with annual-series trend data. This chapter extends the South-Africa-only citation trail (see Citation Trail) to other Bloomberg Philanthropies Data for Health (D4H) Initiative countries, using the same method: search each country’s own national health budgets/policies/ strategic plans for mentions of known burden-of-disease evidence sources — now generalised into a single, cross-country evidence-category taxonomy (see evidence_category_taxonomy in R/constants.R). A second pass (documented in docs/PLAN.md) added annual/recurring publication series for each of the 7 new countries — Uganda’s Annual Health Sector Performance Report, India’s National Health Profile, Rwanda’s annual performance reports, Morocco’s “Santé en Chiffres”, Zambia’s Joint Annual Review, and a Thailand statistics edition — so every country now has >=3 documents spanning several years, enabling a year-over-year trend view below, not just a single snapshot. A follow-up pass also split a new local_surveillance_system evidence category out of the general routine-data-system bucket — named disease- surveillance systems and their own reported counts (e.g. Integrated Disease Surveillance and Response/IDSR, sentinel surveillance) are a genuine evidence product, distinct from general-purpose HMIS/DHIS2 infrastructure. A third pass added real national/health budget documents for the first time — Morocco’s Finance Bill macro/execution report, Rwanda’s Budget Speech and a UNICEF Health Budget Brief, India’s “Demand No. 46” (the Ministry of Health’s own Union Budget appropriation chapter, 3 editions), Kenya’s Programme Based Budget, and Thailand’s Budget in Brief — tagged document_type health_budget/national_budget. A fourth pass specifically re-checked known document sources for missing in-between years: India’s National Health Profile series now runs every year 2018-2023 (2019/2021/2022 added), and Morocco’s “Santé en Chiffres” gained a 2021 edition — both confirmed real, official-domain documents. 52 documents, 8 countries, 1,994 total citation matches (1,518 evidence-source + 476 routine-data-system; since 2026-10-23 routine health-information systems such as DHIS/KHIS/HMIS count as the routine_health_information_system evidence category, leaving 1,922 evidence records plus 72 implementing-agency mentions) — confirmed real document URLs, curl + title-page verified — see docs/KNOWLEDGE.md. This chapter is a landscape view: it does not replicate South Africa’s full numeric budget-panel analysis for these countries, only the document/citation inventory.

Note

Trend findings (still a small sample per country-year, treat as pointers not conclusions): population estimates (census/DHS-family surveys; split on 2026-10-23 into population_estimate_local and population_estimate_international) were the dominant category in almost every country-year in the earlier pooled analysis, with no consistent rise or fall over time within any single country — unlike South Africa’s own GBD share, which rose from 0% (pre-2020) to 12.5% (post-2020) in the existing era comparison. The annual-series documents surfaced three categories the original 2-document snapshot had entirely missed for specific countries: Uganda’s AHSPR editions are the first Uganda documents to name a local_epidemiological_model (the UNAIDS Spectrum tool, narrowly pattern-matched to avoid “autism spectrum disorders” false positives) and the first to show Uganda citing GBD; Rwanda’s two annual reports are the first Rwanda documents with a verbal_autopsy citation (and with 7 matches, the richest verbal-autopsy example in the whole project); and India’s National Health Profile editions are the first India documents citing the decennial Population Census as a named demographic source, alongside a new other-category National Health Accounts citation (also newly found in Uganda and Rwanda). Both Uganda and India’s annual-series documents also reveal heavy reliance on their own routine health-information systems (bare DHIS2/HMIS) — tagged routine_data_system, not evidence_source, consistent with how South Africa’s DHIS/NICD and Kenya’s KHIS/KEMRI are already treated. Separately, a named- surveillance-system category (local_surveillance_system) was split out for 5 countries once IDSR/sentinel-surveillance mentions were checked in context: Uganda (35 matches), India (25), Rwanda (12), Kenya (10), and Zambia (2) all genuinely cite a specific surveillance system or its own reports (e.g. Kenya’s “Weekly IDSR reports are produced on notifiable conditions”) as evidence — not the same as citing bare HMIS/DHIS2 infrastructure, so these are kept as their own evidence category rather than folded into the routine/NA bucket.

Evidence-category mix, by country

Each country’s own total evidence-source citation matches are treated as 100% here, so this compares which evidence categories dominate each country’s own citation practice — not the absolute number of matches, which depends heavily on how many documents/pages are sampled for each country so far (South Africa: 9 documents; India: 8; Morocco: 6; Rwanda: 6; Uganda: 6; Kenya: 5; Thailand: 4; Zambia: 4).

Note

So what: early, small-sample findings (2 documents per country, 4 for Kenya — not yet enough to generalise, but notable):

  1. Zambia’s 2017 and 2022 national strategic plans both name a specific local epidemiological model by name — “Spectrum” (the UNAIDS/Avenir Health HIV demographic-projection tool) — the same role South Africa’s Thembisa model plays, but for a different country’s own HIV programme. Every other country sampled (Rwanda, Thailand, Uganda, India, Kenya) either doesn’t name an equivalent model, or uses the word “spectrum”/“model” only in an unrelated generic sense (“broad-spectrum antibiotics”, “full spectrum of health services”, “across the health spectrum”, management-consulting frameworks like the McKinsey 7S Model) — confirmed independently for Rwanda, India, and Kenya by inspecting each match’s actual context before excluding it, not assumed; the same false positive recurring across three unrelated countries is itself notable.
  2. Three independent countries now have a real named verbal_autopsy citation: Zambia’s 2022-2026 plan (“Sample Vital Registration with Verbal Autopsy”, SAVVY), India’s National Health Mission framework (“verbal autopsy at community level”), and Kenya’s 2018-2023 KHSSP (a costed verbal-autopsy plan integrated into its CRVS system) — the new verbal_autopsy evidence category this cross-country extension added to the original South-Africa-only taxonomy now has three independent real examples.
  3. Kenya’s 2023-2027 KHSSP produced the project’s first real hdss_site citation: a mention that the Kenya Biovax Institute “continues to manage Health and Demographic Surveillance Systems (HDSS) targeting population dynamics.” A modest first example (1 match, no specific site named) rather than a rich one — but real, confirmed by context, not pattern-matched on a false positive. Notably, “Kilifi” (the real KEMRI-Wellcome Trust HDSS site’s home county) appears repeatedly across all 4 Kenya documents, but every instance checked is an ordinary county-name mention (stunting/coverage tables) — not a reference to that research site — so no pattern was built on the county name itself.
  4. A real cross-country false-positive risk was caught and fixed repeatedly: Rwanda’s “RBC” (Rwanda Biomedical Centre) collides with unrelated “RBC” (Red Blood Cell) text in Zambia’s own document; Thailand’s own “DHS” (district health system) is unrelated to the Demographic and Health Survey acronym used by Zambia/Rwanda/Uganda; and Kenya’s “Kilifi” county-name mentions are unrelated to its own real HDSS site of the same name — all three handled by scoping each country’s patterns to its own documents only (see R/extract_app_citations.R’s country filter) and by checking every match’s context before writing a pattern, not a single shared acronym list applied blindly.

Second-pass addendum (annual-series extension): findings 1 and 2 above are no longer unique to Zambia/Zambia+India+Kenya once the annual-series documents are included — see the “Trend findings” callout above for the updated counts (Uganda now also has a local_epidemiological_model and a GBD citation; Rwanda now also has verbal_autopsy, its richest example in the project). Finding 3 (Kenya’s hdss_site) and the Kilifi false-positive catch in finding 4 are unchanged by the new documents. A further follow-up split a new local_surveillance_system category (IDSR/sentinel surveillance) out of the routine-data-system bucket for Uganda, India, Rwanda, Zambia and Kenya — see the “Trend findings” callout above.

  • Three documents still genuinely cite zero matched evidence sources under the current patterns — confirmed by directly checking which document each keyword match actually came from, not assumed: Thailand’s 17th National Health Assembly resolution and Morocco’s “Plan Santé 2025” executive-summary deck (both short policy/summary documents, not a pattern-coverage gap); and Uganda’s Health Service Commission Strategic Plan 2020-2025 (its patterns only matched Uganda’s other documents).   • Uganda’s official Ministry of Health Strategic Plan 2020/21-2024/25 is a confirmed real document, but was not registrable this round: its official host (library.health.go.ug) refused the TLS connection outright, and its WHO-hosted mirror downloaded but failed to parse (corrupted PDF) — both ruled out rather than silently substituted; the two Uganda strategic documents actually used (Health Service Commission Strategic Plan, National Health Compact) are real alternates, not the first choice.
  • Rwanda’s FY2021-22 Annual Health Sector Performance Report was attempted for the annual-series extension but dropped: its official mirror downloaded as a valid, complete PDF but is genuinely corrupted server-side (unrecoverable even after qpdf repair), and the alternate Joint Sector Review mirror found for the same year turned out to be a scanned image PDF with zero extractable text — both ruled out rather than silently substituted with a weaker proxy.
  • Thailand and Zambia’s annual-series searches hit genuine data-availability limits: only 1 additional English-readable Thailand statistics edition could be found (search returned “no more results” after 2 rounds), and only 2 additional Zambia Joint Annual Review editions (2011, 2012) could be found, with no editions located between 2012 and the existing 2017 strategic plan.
  • A fourth pass (2026-10-14) specifically re-checked all 7 new countries’ known document sources for missing in-between years. India’s National Health Profile gap closed completely (2019/2021/2022 found, all official domain — the series now runs every year 2018-2023) and Morocco’s “Santé en Chiffres” gained a 2021 edition (also official domain), narrowing its gap to just 2022-2023. Uganda’s AHSPR FY2023/24 edition, Rwanda’s missing 2013-2017/2021-2022 annual reports, Zambia’s missing 2013-2016/2018-2021 JAR editions, and Morocco’s 2022-2023 Santé en Chiffres editions were all re-searched but not found as direct, workable PDF links — genuine, re-confirmed data-availability gaps (strategic plans like Kenya’s and Zambia’s KHSSP/NHSP are legitimately multi-year documents by design, not a search gap; the AHSPR/NHP/JAR/Santé-en-Chiffres annual series genuinely should exist for every year but aren’t all web-accessible). library.health.go.ug (Uganda’s official MoH document host) was re-tested and remains unreachable (connection refused), consistent with earlier findings.
  • No subnational documents for the new countries yet (national-level only, per the confirmed scope for this phase — see docs/PLAN.md).
  • Real national/health budget documents are now registered for 5 of 7 new countries (Morocco, Rwanda, India, Kenya, Thailand) — still only as document_type metadata for the citation trail, per docs/PLAN.md’s “Budget depth” decision, not parsed for numeric amounts like South Africa’s EPRE panel. Three of these 5 countries’ budget documents (Morocco’s PLF, Rwanda’s Budget Speech, and all 3 editions of India’s Demand No. 46) genuinely cite zero evidence sources — confirmed by inspecting their full extracted text, consistent with being pure financial/establishment-table documents rather than health-sector narratives. No budget document could be found for Uganda (only an Office-of-the-Prime-Minister vote estimate was found, not the Health vote specifically) or Zambia — genuine data-availability gaps, documented rather than forced.
  • All 8 planned D4H countries are now populated with annual-series trend data — the IHEA 2027 World Congress abstract chapter has been updated with the new headline numbers (see docs/PLAN.md).
