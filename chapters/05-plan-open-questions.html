Why CRVS data quality is weak (mechanisms, not this project’s own finding)

Two mechanisms documented in the general CRVS/ICD-coding literature plausibly explain the NCoDV gap (see Overview chapter): (1) an unresolved tension between patient confidentiality and public health surveillance use of HIV-related death data, which plausibly drives under-recording of HIV/AIDS as underlying cause; and (2) non-specific (“ill-defined”) diagnoses written by the certifying clinician, which central ICD coders cannot reliably translate into a specific code regardless of coder skill. Neither mechanism has been tested against this project’s own data — they motivate the two candidate, non-committal recommendation directions below, not a demonstrated result.

Preliminary, non-committal recommendation directions (pending the funding-gap analysis, steps 2-3 above):

  1. Better representation of local burden-of-disease estimates — give domestically-produced corrected estimates (the SAMRC’s own National Burden of Disease Study) more visible standing in the documents that justify budgets, rather than defaulting to registered statistics or an externally-produced alternative (GBD).
  2. Ownership of data quality by the data generator — relocate responsibility for diagnostic data quality to the certifying clinician who writes the diagnosis, since both confidentiality-driven under-recording and non-specific diagnoses originate at certification, not at central coding.

Second-pass review: CRVS, policy and systems lens (planned, not findings)

This expert review (2026-10-21) found five design gaps. The full plan and its sequencing are in docs/PLAN.md.

  1. The exposure is unmeasured. No CRVS quality metric exists yet. The plan is a province × year panel (% ill-defined/garbage codes, completeness, timeliness) built on established frameworks (VSPI, ANACONDA).
  2. The channels are unspecified. Provincial budgets are organised by service platform, not by disease. Burden data most plausibly reaches allocation through the risk adjustment in the provincial equitable-share formula. A counterfactual re-run of that formula with registered vs. corrected burden is the most specific feasible test.
  3. The discretionary margin is small. Compensation of employees is about 64% of FY2024 provincial health appropriations (ad hoc check, not yet in the pipeline). Any effect should be sought in increments and non-personnel lines.
  4. Only differential error matters. Uniform under-registration leaves cause shares unchanged. Only misclassification that differs across causes or provinces can distort relative allocation.
  5. Citation is not use. Citation function (instrumental, conceptual, symbolic) will be coded with an inter-rater reliability check, and “funding gap” is renamed a descriptive burden–allocation divergence.

Future / related projects (out of scope for this thesis)

Scaffold only, not started. A mirror-image companion study asking, for the HIV/TB population this thesis deliberately excludes (see Hypothesis above), whether the dedicated Thembisa epidemiological model is actually cited and correctly drives budget advocacy, and where that budget actually comes from (domestic fiscus vs. PEPFAR/Global Fund donor funding). HIV/TB is excluded from the main funding-gap analysis because it already has a dedicated, purpose-built epidemiological model rather than relying on registered/corrected mortality data the way other causes do — this project asks whether that model’s existence actually translates into well-calibrated budget advocacy, or is cited only nominally.

Draft research questions:

  1. Citation frequency — how often, and in what form, is Thembisa actually cited in South African HIV/TB strategic planning documents (the national HIV/TB/STI NSP, conditional grant business plans, provincial HIV/TB APPs)? (citation_source_patterns in R/extract_app_citations.R already has a working thembisa regex — update (2026-09-24): it returned exactly 1 match across the current 9-document registry (Western Cape’s 2025/26 APP, p59), but none of those 9 documents are HIV/TB-specific — this question is about HIV/TB-dedicated planning documents specifically, still not yet sampled. See the Citation Trail chapter’s registered-mortality-vs-corrected-burden-vs-research section, where Thembisa is one of three tracked research/validation sources.)
  2. Advocacy accuracy — where Thembisa is cited, do the corresponding budget requests/targets actually track its modelled outputs (projected people on ART, new infections, TB incidence/treatment targets), or is it cited as background while budget figures are driven by something else (historical allocation, flat-rate escalation)?
  3. Funding provenance — regardless of (1)-(2), where does HIV/TB budget actually come from (domestic fiscus vs. PEPFAR/Global Fund), and how has that mix shifted given the 2025 PEPFAR/USAID funding disruptions reported in the South African HIV sector?

A prime candidate document is already known and verified: SANAC’s National Strategic Plan on HIV, TB and STIs 2017-2022 (found, downloaded, and confirmed via pdftotext during the main thesis’s pre-2020 citation hunt, then excluded from the main registry as out-of-scope — wrong organisation, disease-specific). Its current-edition successor would need to be located at sanac.org.za (not yet checked). Other not-yet-fetched sources needed: HIV/TB conditional grant business plans (Division of Revenue Act annexes), PEPFAR South Africa Country Operational Plan documents, and Global Fund grant/disbursement data for South Africa.

If started, this should get its own registry/scripts (reusing the existing extract_source_citations()/build_citation_trail() functions rather than duplicating them) and its own PLAN/KNOWLEDGE-style register, kept separate from the main thesis so a positive-control or sustainability finding here doesn’t get blended with the main CRVS argument.

Open design questions (from docs/PLAN.md)

  • What’s the right unit of comparison for “implied budget share” vs. “burden-corrected share” — rand amount, per-capita, or share-of-total-health-budget?
  • How to handle provinces/years where NCoDV-style correction factors don’t exist at the required granularity (NCoDV fieldwork was 2017-18 only) — extrapolate, or restrict the funding-gap analysis to that period?
  • How to align Stats SA report years (data year vs. publication year) with budget financial years for a coherent panel index.
  • Whether GBD estimates should be used as a validation check only, or blended into the corrected-burden figure alongside NCoDV factors.
